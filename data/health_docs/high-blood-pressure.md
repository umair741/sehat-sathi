# High Blood Pressure (Hypertension)

Also called: Hypertension, HBP

Source: MedlinePlus (US National Library of Medicine) - https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000468.htm

## What is high blood pressure?

Blood pressure is a measurement of the force exerted against the walls of your arteries as your heart pumps blood to your body. Hypertension is the medical term used to describe high blood pressure.

Untreated high blood pressure can lead to many medical problems, including heart disease, stroke, kidney failure, eye problems, and other health issues.

Blood pressure readings are given as two numbers. The top number is called systolic blood pressure. The bottom number is diastolic blood pressure. For example, 120 over 80 (written as 120/80 mm Hg).

- Normal blood pressure: lower than 120/80 mm Hg most of the time
- Elevated blood pressure: top number 120 to 129 mm Hg with bottom number less than 80 mm Hg
- High blood pressure (hypertension): one or both readings are 130/80 mm Hg or higher most of the time
- Stage 2 high blood pressure: 140/90 mm Hg or higher

## Why does it happen? (Causes)

Many factors can affect blood pressure, including:

- The amount of water and salt in your body
- The condition of your kidneys, nervous system, or blood vessels
- Your hormone levels

You are more likely to be told your blood pressure is too high as you get older, because your blood vessels become stiffer with age. High blood pressure increases your chance of having a stroke, heart attack, heart failure, kidney disease, or early death.

Most of the time, no cause is found - this is called essential hypertension.

High blood pressure caused by another medical condition or medicine is called secondary hypertension. It may be due to:

- Chronic kidney disease
- Disorders of the adrenal gland (such as pheochromocytoma or Cushing syndrome)
- Hyperparathyroidism
- Pregnancy or preeclampsia
- Medicines such as birth control pills, diet pills, some cold medicines, migraine medicines, corticosteroids, some antipsychotics, and certain medicines used to treat cancer
- Narrowed artery that supplies blood to the kidney (renal artery stenosis)
- Obstructive sleep apnea

## Who is more likely to get it?

You have a higher risk for high blood pressure if you:

- Are older
- Are obese
- Are often stressed or anxious
- Drink too much alcohol (more than 1 drink per day for women and more than 2 for men)
- Eat too much salt
- Have a family history of high blood pressure
- Have diabetes
- Smoke or use tobacco

## What are the symptoms?

Most of the time, there are NO symptoms - this is why high blood pressure is called the "silent killer." For most people, it is found when they visit their provider or have it checked elsewhere. People can develop heart disease and kidney problems without knowing they have high blood pressure.

Malignant hypertension is a dangerous form of very high blood pressure. Symptoms may include:

- Severe headache
- Nausea and vomiting
- Confusion
- Vision changes
- Nosebleeds

## How is it diagnosed?

Your provider will measure your blood pressure several times before diagnosing you, because it is normal for blood pressure to vary based on the time of day.

All adults age 18 or older should have their blood pressure checked periodically - once per year is recommended for people age 40 and older.

Home monitoring: blood pressure readings taken at home may be a better measure than those taken at your provider's office.

- Use a good quality, well-fitting home monitor with a properly sized cuff
- Be relaxed and seated for five or more minutes before taking a reading
- Bring your home monitor to appointments so your provider can verify it works correctly

Tests may also be done to look for:

- High cholesterol
- Heart disease, using tests such as an echocardiogram or electrocardiogram
- Diabetes and kidney disease, using blood tests, urinalysis, or ultrasound of the kidneys

## What is the treatment? (Solution)

The goal of treatment is to lower blood pressure to reduce the risk of health problems.

Elevated blood pressure: lifestyle changes; medicines are rarely used at this stage.

Stage 1 high blood pressure (130/80 to under 140/90): lifestyle changes, and medicines depending on your other risk factors.

Stage 2 high blood pressure (140/90 or higher): usually medicines together with lifestyle changes.

LIFESTYLE CHANGES:

- Eat a heart-healthy diet, including potassium and fiber
- Drink plenty of water
- Get at least 150 minutes of moderate to vigorous aerobic exercise per week
- If you smoke or use tobacco, quit
- Limit alcohol to 1 drink a day for women and 2 for men, or less
- Limit sodium (salt) - aim for less than 1,500 mg per day
- Reduce stress - try meditation or yoga
- Stay at a healthy body weight

MEDICINES: There are many different medicines to treat high blood pressure. Often a single medicine is not enough, and two or more may be needed. It is very important to take prescribed medicines - if you have side effects, tell your provider so a different medicine can be substituted.

## Outlook (Prognosis)

Most of the time, high blood pressure can be controlled with medicine and lifestyle changes.

When blood pressure is not well controlled, you are at risk for:

- Bleeding from the aorta (the large blood vessel supplying the abdomen, pelvis, and legs)
- Chronic kidney disease
- Heart attack and heart failure
- Poor blood supply to the legs
- Problems with vision
- Stroke

## Can it be prevented?

Many people can prevent or delay high blood pressure by following the same lifestyle changes used to treat it: a heart-healthy, low-salt diet; healthy weight; regular exercise; limited alcohol; not smoking; and managing stress.

## When to see a doctor

- Have regular check-ups and get your blood pressure checked, especially if someone in your family has or had high blood pressure
- If you have high blood pressure, have regular follow-up visits with your provider
- Contact your provider right away if home monitoring shows your blood pressure is still high
- Seek urgent care for severe headache, vision changes, confusion, or nosebleeds with very high readings (possible malignant hypertension)
