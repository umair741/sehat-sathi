# Dengue

Also called: Break-bone fever, Dengue fever

Source: MedlinePlus (US National Library of Medicine) / CDC - https://medlineplus.gov/dengue.html

## What is dengue?

Dengue is a viral infection. It can be caused by four related viruses. You can get it if an infected mosquito bites you. It is common in warm, wet areas of the world, and outbreaks are more common during rainy seasons.

About one in four people infected with dengue will get sick. For those who do get sick, the infection can be mild or severe.

## Why does it happen? (Causes)

Dengue spreads to people through the bite of an infected mosquito. It does NOT spread directly from person to person. However:

- The infection can be passed to the baby during pregnancy
- In rare cases, it can spread through a blood transfusion, organ transplant, or needle stick injury (an accidental injury from a used needle)

## What are the symptoms?

The symptoms of dengue usually last 2 to 7 days and include:

- A high fever
- Nausea and vomiting
- Rash
- Aches and pains (eye pain, usually behind the eyes, and pain in the muscles, joints, or bones)

Dengue is sometimes called "break-bone fever" because of the severe muscle, joint, and bone pain it can cause.

## Severe dengue (medical emergency)

Severe dengue is a serious form of the illness. About 1 in 20 people who get sick with dengue will develop severe dengue. It can cause shock, internal bleeding, and even death.

People are more likely to develop severe dengue if they:

- Have had dengue before
- Are pregnant
- Are infants

Warning signs of severe dengue usually begin about 24 to 48 hours AFTER the fever has gone away. If you or a family member have these signs, get immediate medical care:

- Severe abdominal (belly) pain and tenderness
- Vomiting at least 3 times in 24 hours
- Bleeding from the nose or gums
- Vomiting blood, or blood in the stool
- Feeling tired, restless, or irritable

## How is it diagnosed?

There is a blood test which can show if you have been infected with dengue. If you have symptoms, see a health care provider and mention any recent travel to areas where dengue is common.

## What is the treatment? (Solution)

There is no specific medicine to treat dengue. Most people will get better after about a week. But if you think you might have dengue:

- See a health care provider and mention any recent travel
- Rest as much as possible
- Take acetaminophen to control fever and relieve pain
- Do NOT take aspirin or ibuprofen, because they increase the risk of bleeding
- Drink plenty of fluids to prevent dehydration

Severe dengue often requires care at a hospital.

## Outlook (Prognosis)

Most people with mild dengue recover within about a week with rest and fluids. Severe dengue is a medical emergency that requires immediate hospital care - with prompt treatment most people survive, but delayed care can be fatal.

## Can it be prevented?

A dengue vaccine is approved in some countries. In the United States, it is approved for children ages 9 to 16 who have previously had dengue and live in areas where dengue is common.

You can also help prevent dengue by avoiding mosquito bites:

- Wear insect repellent (such as repellent with DEET or another EPA-registered repellent), following the instructions on the product
- Wear clothes that cover your arms, legs, and feet
- Close unscreened doors and windows

## When to see a doctor

- See a provider if you have a fever with rash, severe headache, pain behind the eyes, or severe body aches, especially during or after the rainy season, or after travel to an area where dengue is common
- Get IMMEDIATE medical care for warning signs of severe dengue: severe abdominal pain, repeated vomiting (3 or more times in 24 hours), bleeding from the nose or gums, blood in vomit or stool, or extreme tiredness, restlessness, or irritability - especially in the 24 to 48 hours after the fever goes away
